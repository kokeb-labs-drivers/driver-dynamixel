Hardware¶

How a DYNAMIXEL servo bus is wired and timed, as the driver uses it: the connector, the half-duplex data line, the bus settings, the timing and the supply.

The driver talks to a family of servos, not to one chip. The reference part of this page is the XM430-W350; the other families differ in the connector housing and the supply range (Control tables). Not verified on hardware: everything here follows the ROBOTIS e-Manual, which is a set of web pages (see the Datasheet map).

Datasheet sections this page relies on
  • Half duplex line and Tx/Rx direction: ROBOTIS e-Manual, DYNAMIXEL Protocol 2.0 web page, no revision mark, 1.4 Half Duplex; 1.5 Tx, Rx Direction, p. 1
  • Specifications, baud rates and ID range: ROBOTIS e-Manual, XM430-W350 web page, no revision mark, 1 Specifications, p. 1
  • Connector, power and communication circuit: ROBOTIS e-Manual, XM430-W350 web page, no revision mark, 4.5 Connector Information; 4.6 Communication Circuit, p. 1
  • Connector and half-duplex circuit, protocol 1.0 servos: ROBOTIS e-Manual, AX-12A web page, no revision mark, 5.2 Connector Information; 5.4 Communication Circuit, p. 1

All of them, with the driver API for each: Datasheet map.

The servos need their own supply

The XM430-W350 runs from 10.0 V to 14.8 V (12.0 V recommended) and draws amperes when it moves. Never power it from the MCU board. The TTL connector carries a single TTL data line (the e-Manual's text gives no voltage level; its circuit figure pulls the line up to 5 V, see Circuits below); the RS-485 variant is differential and needs a transceiver.

Pins¶

The 3-pin TTL connector of the X series (JST EH) carries ground, the supply and one half-duplex data line. The RS-485 variant has the same first two pins and splits the data into DATA+ and DATA-.

The driver has no pin of its own to drive: the only hooks are Write() and Read() of your UART port. The direction of the half-duplex stage is part of Write() (Porting).

The data line¶

All servos and the master share one data line. The master sends an instruction packet; the addressed servo answers with a status packet after its Return Delay Time (500 microseconds at the factory value, up to 508). Between the two the line must be turned round: the master stops driving it and listens.

Wiring How the direction is switched Port Config::echo
UART with a direction pin (RS-485 transceiver, tri-state buffer) The UART's RTS output or a GPIO, high while sending Write() raises it, sends, waits for the last stop bit and lowers it None
UART with a hardware half-duplex mode (ESP32 UART_MODE_RS485_HALF_DUPLEX) The hardware, with the first start bit and after the last stop bit Write() sends and waits for the transmitter to be done None
TX and RX tied to the single wire (open-drain or diode stage) None: the master's own bytes come back on RX Write() leaves the echo in the receive FIFO Loopback

With EchoMode::Loopback the driver reads the echo of every packet it sent and compares it with the request: another talker on the line, or a short, shows as Error::EchoMismatch before any reply is waited for.

Bus settings¶

Setting Value
UART format 8 data bits, no parity, 1 stop bit
Bit rate About 7 800 (AX-12A) or 9 600 to 4 500 000 bit/s on the catalogued families; factory 57 600 (X series) or 1 000 000 (protocols 1.0 families); every servo on the bus must run the same rate
Packet Protocol 1.0 or 2.0, the same for every servo on the bus (Protocol)
Byte gap inside an instruction At most 1.5 ms in protocol 2.0 and 100 ms in protocol 1.0, or the servo drops the packet: Write() sends the packet in one go
IDs 0 to 252 (2.0) or 0 to 253 (1.0); 254 is the broadcast ID; every ID on a bus must be unique

The factory bit rate and ID differ by family: an X-series servo comes at ID 1 and 57 600 bit/s, an AX-12A at ID 1 and 1 000 000 bit/s. Config::baud must match; a wrong rate looks like silence (Error::Timeout) or noise.

Circuits¶

Figure description and sources

A schematic. The MCU is on the left, each of its pins level with the chip pin it connects to; above each wire is the hook of the driver's port that drives or reads it. Supply pins point up to their supply and decoupling, ground pins down. Pins on the right carry the loads and the pins tied to a supply or ground; a cross marks a pin left open. A dot is a junction; wires that cross without a dot are not connected.

Drawn from: docs/hardware.yml; inc/dynamixel_uart_interface.hpp.

The e-Manual's recommended TTL circuit is a pair of tri-state buffers (a 74LVC2G241 in the figure), one for each direction. One direction signal (TX_Enable) drives both enables: low enables the receive buffer (servo DATA to the MCU's RXD), high enables the transmit buffer (the MCU's TXD to DATA). The circuit is a figure only: the page text says that it "is designed for 5V or 5V tolerant MCU. Otherwise, use a Level Shifter to match the voltage of MCU", and the figure runs the buffers from 5 V. It has a 10 kilohm pull-up to 5 V on the RXD node and on the DATA node, and a 10 kilohm pull-down to ground on TX_Enable, so that the stage listens while the MCU pin is not driven. Do not copy the enable resistor as a pull-up: a pull-up on the direction pin would enable the transmit buffer and drive DATA high at idle, against the servos' own answers. A 3.3 V MCU therefore needs a level shifter; the e-Manual shows no 3.3 V circuit. What is read from the figure (the part name, the 5 V supply, the pull-ups and the pull-down) is unverified against a second source. The stage drawn here is a block, not a part; any circuit that gives the same behaviour (an RS-485 transceiver with the right level, or a UART's own half-duplex mode) works the same way for the driver. Add a bulk capacitor on the servo supply; the 100 microfarad of the drawing is an example, not a datasheet value.

Timing¶

Parameter Value Driver
Return Delay Time 500 microseconds at the factory value, at most 508 (register, 2 microsecond counts) kTurnaroundUs, kMaxTurnaroundUs; Config::reply_timeout_us must be longer
Reply wait Not specified by the e-Manual Config::reply_timeout_us, 20 ms by default (100 microseconds to 1 s)
Wait inside a packet Not specified Config::gap_timeout_us, 5 ms by default
Reboot, restore, factory reset The servo restarts after its reply Config::reboot_wait_us, 500 ms by default
Baud rate change The servo switches after the reply Config::baud_change_wait_us, 10 ms by default
Broadcast ping The servos answer in ID order Config::broadcast_timeout_us per reply, 100 ms by default

A byte takes ten bit times: 174 microseconds at 57 600 bit/s, 10 microseconds at 1 Mbit/s. A Fast Sync Read of many servos at a low rate needs a reply_timeout_us that covers the whole packet; the driver waits per read, not for the whole packet (Configuration).

Power¶

Quantity XM430-W350
Supply voltage 10.0 V to 14.8 V, 12.0 V recommended
Stall current 2.1 A at 11.1 V, 2.3 A at 12.0 V
Standby current 40 mA

The servo reports its input voltage in 0.1 V counts (Reg::PresentInputVoltage; it shows in ReadFeedback()). Other families run from 5 V to 12 V or more; check the e-Manual page of the model.
